How to draw... an open safety pin

Build the coil and diagonal spine first, then raise the open point and fold the clasp around the opposite end. This 20-minute pencil sketch practices a thin metal silhouette and restrained shadow.

20 minEasy
Start drawing

Pencil ready?

Let's draw an open safety pin

Treat this as one small practice round: build the subject lightly, notice the shapes, then darken only the lines that help the finished sketch.

Grab your stuff

Materials

  • Drawing paperWhatever you already have
  • HB pencilWhatever you already have
  • 2B pencilWhatever you already have
  • Kneaded eraserCompletely optional

No colored pencil? Graphite alone makes a complete sketch.

  1. 01

    Draw the spine and coil

    Draw a long diagonal spine down-right and loop its lower end into one complete round coil.

    Sketch tip: Make the coil fully round before raising the point.

  2. 02

    Raise the open point

    From the coil, pull a straight slim arm upward and taper it to a point, leaving a wide gap to the spine top.

    Sketch tip: Keep the two arms apart so the pin reads as open.

  3. 03

    Fold the clasp

    At the top end of the spine, draw a folded hook clasp with its opening facing the pointed arm.

    Sketch tip: Face the clasp opening toward the point.

  4. 04

    Leave metal glints

    Add two short parallel glints inside the clasp and one tiny curved glint inside the coil.

    Sketch tip: Leave the three glints as pale paper.

  5. 05

    Set the ground shadow

    Shade a broken horizontal shadow below the pin, darkest under the coil.

    Sketch tip: Keep the shadow broken and quiet.

  6. 06

    Shade the metal

    Strengthen the established spine, arm, coil, and clasp with 2B graphite, leaving the existing glints pale and deepening the existing ground shadow.

    Sketch tip: Stop while the graphite still looks like pencil on paper.
